Shape cleanup

On, insect damage and other perforations are closed up. Off, they stay open and print as through-holes.

Rounds the outline into real curves by filtering it, rather than by deleting corners — a mask is a staircase of square pixels, and this is what turns that staircase into a leaf edge. Raise it to iron out scanning noise; too far and the lobes lose their tips. Applies to the leaf outline only, never to the lettering or the veins.

Drops vertices the shape does not need, which is purely a file-size control — every vertex becomes two wall triangles. Above roughly 1 px it starts flattening the curve back into straight chords. Smoothing 0 with simplify 1.5 reproduces the original generate_stl_from_mask behaviour exactly.

Geometry

Lettering

Thin strokes do not survive a 0.4 mm nozzle. Thickening fattens every stroke evenly, which keeps a delicate face legible in print.

Download

Engraved lettering is cut into the face as one solid. Raised lettering is exported as its own shell resting on the face — slicers merge the two, and printing it in a second colour is just a filament change at that layer.

Vein extraction

Signal

Green usually separates veins best on a pressed leaf: chlorophyll absorbs red and blue, so the lamina is brightest — and most even — in green.

A vein is only a few grey levels darker than the tissue beside it, but the lamina's own brightness varies far more across the leaf — so what gets thresholded is the difference from a LOCAL average, not the grey value. Set this to about twice the widest vein you want to keep.

Linear features

Probes the image with a straight line in several directions and keeps what survives. Blotches and areole texture die; veins live. It earns its keep at a LOW threshold — at a high one it mostly deletes the secondary veins.

Threshold

Otsu splits the enhanced image at the level that best separates two populations. It is a good starting point, but it always finds a split — even on an image with no veins in it.

Cleanup

A vein thinner than the nozzle will not print. Thickening every stroke evenly is the cheapest way to keep the fine venation.

The leaf's own outline is the strongest dark line in the photo. Insetting the search keeps it from being traced as a vein.
